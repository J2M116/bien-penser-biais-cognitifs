# Affect Heuristic

## Short

Our immediate emotions shape our assessment of a risk or benefit before we fully examine the facts.

## Example

A claim made by a likable person seems more credible, even when the evidence presented is weak.

## Detailed description

Affect serves as a shortcut: a positive impression tends to reduce perceived risk and increase perceived benefit, and vice versa. This mechanism can be useful for making quick decisions, but it becomes misleading when the emotion is not relevant to the question.

## Why it matters

It operates in health, finance, politics, communication, and risk assessment.

## Limits and nuances

An emotion is not an error in itself; the bias arises when it replaces relevant information.

## Prevention

- Name the emotion you are feeling before reaching a conclusion.
- Assess the evidence, probabilities, and consequences separately.
