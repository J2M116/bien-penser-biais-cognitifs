# Anchoring Effect

## Short

An initial value or piece of information exerts too much influence on a later estimate.

## Example

After hearing that a project will cost one million euros, subsequent estimates remain close to that amount, even if the initial estimate was arbitrary.

## Detailed description

The anchor provides a starting point from which adjustment often remains insufficient. The effect appears even when the initial value is not very informative and can persist among experienced people.

## Why it matters

It affects negotiations, prices, forecasts, diagnoses, timelines, and the interpretation of incorrect numbers.

## Limits and nuances

The size of the effect depends on the task, the available knowledge, and the anchor's apparent relevance.

## Prevention

- Produce an independent estimate before seeing anyone else's.
- Actively look for reasons why the anchor might be too high or too low.
