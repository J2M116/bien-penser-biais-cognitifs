# Attentional Bias

## Short

Attention is drawn disproportionately to certain cues at the expense of equally relevant information.

## Example

After a series of alarming news stories, a person notices mainly signs of danger in an otherwise balanced report.

## Detailed description

Concerns, goals, emotions, and previous experiences shape what is noticed and what is ignored. Content that attention does not select may never contribute to the final judgment.

## Why it matters

This mechanism operates very early in reasoning and can therefore distort every stage that follows.

## Limits and nuances

There are several attentional biases; findings observed for threats and anxiety do not automatically generalize to every topic.

## Prevention

- Use a checklist that requires examining predefined categories of information.
- Make a second pass devoted to elements that were initially less salient.
