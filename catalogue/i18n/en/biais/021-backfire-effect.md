# Backfire Effect

## Short

Under certain conditions, a correction may strengthen the misconception it is intended to reduce.

## Example

A person with a strong political commitment rejects a threatening refutation and then asserts their position even more forcefully.

## Detailed description

The hypothesis proposes that a correction incompatible with a person's identity or convictions triggers a defensive response. However, recent studies generally find that corrections improve accuracy and that genuine backfire effects are rare and context-dependent.

## Why it matters

The issue matters for corrective communication, but its practical importance is often overstated.

## Limits and nuances

Never claim that correcting an error normally reinforces it: that is precisely the contested point.

## Prevention

- Correct the error clearly, with an alternative explanation and a credible source.
- Measure the correction's actual effect instead of assuming a defensive response.
