# Bandwagon Effect

## Short

The apparent popularity of an idea or choice increases our tendency to adopt it.

## Example

An opinion receives more approval after a large number of positive votes is displayed.

## Detailed description

Other people's behavior can be interpreted as information about quality or as a social norm to follow. Rankings, counters, and polls make this signal especially visible.

## Why it matters

It influences consumption, elections, markets, social networks, and the spread of beliefs.

## Limits and nuances

Following the group can be rational when others genuinely have independent information; the bias arises when popularity is mistaken for truth.

## Prevention

- Form and record your judgment before seeing votes or rankings.
- Check whether the observed choices are independent or copied from one another.
