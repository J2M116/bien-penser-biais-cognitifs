# Base Rate Fallacy

## Short

We underuse the general frequency of an event in favor of a striking individual case.

## Example

A highly accurate medical test seems almost certain to be right, without taking into account that the disease is extremely rare.

## Detailed description

A correct probabilistic judgment combines the base rate with the diagnostic value of the specific information. Descriptions based on resemblance or emotion can make us overlook this prior frequency.

## Why it matters

The consequences are significant in medicine, law, security, hiring, and the interpretation of tests.

## Limits and nuances

Performance changes substantially depending on whether information is presented as probabilities or natural frequencies.

## Prevention

- Write down the base rate explicitly before examining the individual case.
- Convert percentages into natural frequencies, for example out of 1,000 cases.
