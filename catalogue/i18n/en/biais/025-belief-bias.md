# Belief Bias

## Short

The plausibility of a conclusion influences our judgment of the reasoning's logical validity.

## Example

An invalid syllogism is accepted because its conclusion matches a true or familiar belief.

## Detailed description

We can confuse two questions: is the conclusion believable, and does it actually follow from the premises? The bias becomes visible when logic and belief lead to opposite answers.

## Why it matters

It directly affects argumentation, fact-checking, and debates in which the conclusion already suits us.

## Limits and nuances

It is studied mainly through formal reasoning tasks; its extent in ordinary conversation varies with the task.

## Prevention

- First assess the structure of the argument by replacing its content with neutral terms.
- Explicitly separate the truth of the premises, the validity of the reasoning, and the truth of the conclusion.
