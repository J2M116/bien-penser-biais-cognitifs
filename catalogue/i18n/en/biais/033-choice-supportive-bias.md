# Choice-supportive Bias

## Short

After making a decision, we reconstruct the chosen option more favorably and the alternatives less favorably.

## Example

After buying a phone, its owner remembers mainly its strengths and exaggerates the flaws of the model they rejected.

## Detailed description

Memory for an option's features can be distorted in ways that support the decision-maker's identity and reduce regret. Positive qualities may even be wrongly attributed to the chosen option.

## Why it matters

This bias complicates learning from experience, comparing products, and revisiting past decisions.

## Limits and nuances

It does not mean that all satisfaction after a choice is irrational; a distortion relative to the information available at the outset must be demonstrated.

## Prevention

- Keep a record of the criteria and information used before the decision.
- During review, assess the strengths and weaknesses of every option symmetrically.
