# Confirmation Bias

## Short

We preferentially seek, interpret, or remember information that confirms our existing beliefs.

## Example

When evaluating a dietary supplement, a person consults only favorable testimonials and dismisses negative results as exceptions.

## Detailed description

The bias can affect information seeking, the interpretation of ambiguous evidence, the weighting of evidence, and memory. It protects established beliefs and can interact with source selection and social identity.

## Why it matters

Its reach extends across science, medicine, management, relationships, politics, and the use of search engines.

## Limits and nuances

The term covers several processes; favoring one hypothesis is not always biased when prior probabilities justify it.

## Prevention

- State in advance what evidence would refute the hypothesis.
- Seek out the strongest contradictory evidence and compare independent sources.
