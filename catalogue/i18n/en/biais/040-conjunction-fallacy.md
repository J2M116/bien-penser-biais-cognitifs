# Conjunction Fallacy

## Short

A combination of two events can sometimes seem more likely than one of those events considered on its own.

## Example

A detailed profile makes “bank employee and activist” seem more plausible than “bank employee,” even though the first set is contained within the second.

## Detailed description

The representativeness of a coherent scenario can outweigh the rule that an intersection cannot be more probable than either of its components. Added details make the story more compelling while mathematically reducing its probability.

## Why it matters

It matters in diagnoses, causal scenarios, forecasts, and complex conspiracy narratives.

## Limits and nuances

How often the error occurs depends strongly on the wording and representation of the problem.

## Prevention

- Draw the sets or restate the problem using frequencies.
- Explicitly compare the simple event with the same event plus an additional condition.
