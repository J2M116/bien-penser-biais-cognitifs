# Conservatism Bias

## Short

We revise a belief too little when new evidence should lead to a larger change in our judgment.

## Example

After several economic indicators contradict a forecast, an analyst reduces their initial confidence only very slightly.

## Detailed description

The bias concerns the updating of an already formed belief: new information is taken into account, but given too little weight. It differs from confirmation bias, which can also determine what information is sought or accepted.

## Why it matters

It can prolong poor strategies, diagnoses, and forecasts despite accumulating evidence to the contrary.

## Limits and nuances

Normative updating depends on the reliability of the new evidence; cautious revision is therefore not automatically biased.

## Prevention

- Assign an expected weight to different possible outcomes in advance.
- Periodically reassess the situation as though you were taking over the case today.
