# Courtesy Bias

## Short

We give a more positive or socially acceptable response to avoid offending the person we are speaking with.

## Example

A patient reports being very satisfied when the healthcare professional who treated them asks the question directly.

## Detailed description

The interviewer's presence, identity, or perceived expectations can alter a response. The mechanism is close to social desirability bias, but places particular emphasis on politeness and the relationship with the other person.

## Why it matters

It can distort satisfaction surveys, interviews, managerial evaluations, and the collection of feedback.

## Limits and nuances

The evidence is context-dependent and sometimes mixed; this is also a measurement bias produced by the survey situation.

## Prevention

- Collect responses anonymously and away from the person being evaluated.
- Use neutral wording that makes criticism explicitly acceptable.
