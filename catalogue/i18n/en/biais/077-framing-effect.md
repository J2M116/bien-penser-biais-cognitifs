# Framing Effect

## Short

Logically equivalent formulations can lead to different judgments or choices.

## Example

A treatment accepted by 90% of patients seems preferable to the same treatment described as failing in 10% of cases.

## Detailed description

Framing selects a reference point, a gain, a loss, or a particular aspect of the situation. The decision can then change even though the objective consequences being described are identical.

## Why it matters

It affects health, public policy, insurance, negotiation, marketing, and the presentation of statistics.

## Limits and nuances

The aliases “Frequency Illusion” and “Baader-Meinhof Phenomenon” given in the source appendix are incorrect and should not be treated as synonyms.

## Prevention

- Restate the same information in terms of gains, losses, and absolute values.
- Present both equivalent frames at the same time.
