# Google Effect

## Short

When we know information will remain available online, we remember where to find it more readily than we remember its content.

## Example

A person forgets a figure they read a few minutes earlier but remembers precisely which search query will retrieve it.

## Detailed description

External resources can become a form of transactive memory. This changes encoding effort and the type of information retained, without simply meaning that the Internet destroys memory.

## Why it matters

The phenomenon affects learning, information seeking, digital work, and reliance on accessible tools.

## Limits and nuances

Effects vary across tasks and replications; remembering how to access information can be an adaptive strategy.

## Prevention

- Decide which information must be understood and remembered before beginning the search.
- Practice retrieval without access to a search engine for essential knowledge.
