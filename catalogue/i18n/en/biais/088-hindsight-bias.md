# Hindsight Bias

## Short

After learning an outcome, we judge it to have been more predictable than it actually was beforehand.

## Example

After a project fails, everyone claims the warning signs were obvious from the start.

## Detailed description

Knowing the outcome alters the memory of initial predictions, strengthens the apparent coherence of the causes, and reduces the alternatives we imagine. This can create an illusion of predictability.

## Why it matters

It distorts learning, audits, medicine, justice, history, and the evaluation of decision-makers.

## Limits and nuances

An event may have been objectively predictable; the bias is demonstrated by the difference between judgments made before and after the outcome, not by a retrospective claim alone.

## Prevention

- Record predictions, probabilities, and reasons before learning the outcome.
- Reconstruct plausible alternative scenarios using only the information available at the time.
