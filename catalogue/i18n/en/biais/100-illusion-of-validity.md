# Illusion of Validity

## Short

The apparent coherence of information makes us excessively confident in a judgment or prediction.

## Example

A recruiter becomes very confident in an assessment because a candidate's story is fluent and coherent, despite having little predictive data.

## Detailed description

A set of consistent cues produces a convincing story, even when the cues are redundant, selected, or only weakly related to the outcome. Subjective confidence then exceeds the method's actual validity.

## Why it matters

This mechanism affects interviews, forecasts, diagnoses, intelligence analysis, and explanatory narratives.

## Limits and nuances

Coherence can sometimes be legitimate evidence; it should be compared with observed predictive accuracy and with the independence of the cues.

## Prevention

- Measure the method's past accuracy rather than relying on its plausibility alone.
- Check whether the cues are independent and look for data that could support a competing account.
