# Illusory Correlation

## Short

We perceive a relationship between two variables when it is absent or much weaker than it appears.

## Example

A few memorable incidents are enough to associate a minority group with a negative behavior that is actually just as common elsewhere.

## Detailed description

The co-occurrence of rare or salient events attracts attention and is remembered better than the many ordinary cases. Without a complete table of all four possible combinations, an association appears stronger than it is.

## Why it matters

It fuels stereotypes, pseudoscience, medical errors, and false causal explanations.

## Limits and nuances

A genuine correlation may exist; proper evaluation requires comparative data, a denominator, and a measure of uncertainty.

## Prevention

- Build a table that includes both the presence and absence of each variable.
- Calculate the association across all observations rather than focusing on memorable cases.
