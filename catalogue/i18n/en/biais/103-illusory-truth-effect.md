# Illusory Truth Effect

## Short

Repeating a statement makes it feel truer, even when no new evidence is provided.

## Example

A false slogan seems more credible after appearing several times in different news feeds.

## Detailed description

Repetition increases familiarity and processing fluency; this ease can be mistakenly interpreted as a sign of truth. The effect can influence both plausible statements and claims already known to be questionable.

## Why it matters

It is central to advertising, propaganda, rumors, misinformation, and the way corrections are remembered.

## Limits and nuances

The average effect is robust, but its size depends on delay, prior knowledge, context, and wording.

## Prevention

- Avoid unnecessarily repeating the exact wording of a false claim.
- Emphasize the correct fact and remind readers of the quality of its source.
