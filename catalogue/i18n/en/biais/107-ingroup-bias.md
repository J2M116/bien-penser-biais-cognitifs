# Ingroup Bias

## Short

We evaluate or favor members of a group we identify with more positively.

## Example

The same mistake is excused when made by a colleague on one's own team but treated as evidence of incompetence when made by someone on a rival team.

## Detailed description

Simple categorization can be enough to alter allocation, trust, and judgment. The bias can take the form of favoritism toward one's own group without necessarily producing explicit hostility toward others.

## Why it matters

It affects politics, hiring, cooperation, justice, sports, and the partisan circulation of information.

## Limits and nuances

Its strength depends on the salience, status, and relevance of the group; not every preference for someone close to us is unjustified.

## Prevention

- Use identical criteria defined before learning a person's group membership.
- Have decisions reviewed by people with different affiliations or perspectives.
