# Just-world Hypothesis

## Short

We tend to believe that people get what they deserve, even when chance or structural factors play a major role.

## Example

A fraud victim is blamed for being naive in order to preserve the belief that cautious people are safe.

## Detailed description

Belief in a just order can provide a sense of control and predictability. When an injustice is difficult to remedy, it can lead people to reinterpret the victim or the outcome as deserving what happened.

## Why it matters

It influences moral judgment, views of poverty and health, justice, responses to victims, and the legitimization of institutions.

## Limits and nuances

The concept can also be measured as a stable belief; its effects depend on context and are not always the same as victim blaming.

## Prevention

- Separate causal responsibility, moral responsibility, and the possibility of prevention.
- Explicitly examine the roles of chance, constraints, and unequal starting conditions.
