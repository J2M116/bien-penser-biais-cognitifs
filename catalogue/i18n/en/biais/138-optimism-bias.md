# Optimism Bias

## Short

We underestimate our future risks and overestimate the probability of favorable outcomes.

## Example

A driver believes that accidents mainly happen to other people and neglects a safety measure.

## Detailed description

Optimism can concern an expected outcome or a comparison with others. It can sometimes support motivation and perseverance, but it distorts preparation, prevention, and resource allocation when risks are objectively underestimated.

## Why it matters

It plays a role in health, safety, planning, entrepreneurship, and climate change.

## Limits and nuances

Optimism is not always an error and can be adaptive; a forecast must be compared with an appropriate reference risk.

## Prevention

- Use base rates from comparable people or projects.
- Prepare a failure plan and identify early warning signs of drift.
