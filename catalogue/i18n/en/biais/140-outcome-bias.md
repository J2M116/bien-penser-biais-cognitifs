# Outcome Bias

## Short

We judge the quality of a decision by its outcome rather than by the information available when the choice was made.

## Example

A prudent medical decision is called poor because a rare complication occurred.

## Detailed description

A sound procedure can lead to a bad outcome by chance, while a poor procedure can succeed through luck. Confusing decisions with outcomes prevents proper learning and can reward unjustified risk-taking.

## Why it matters

It affects medicine, investment, management, justice, sports, and policy evaluation.

## Limits and nuances

An outcome can provide relevant information about the quality of a rule; the bias lies in giving it unjustified weight when evaluating a single past decision.

## Prevention

- Evaluate the process, information, predicted probabilities, and outcome separately.
- Have the decision assessed before revealing its outcome.
