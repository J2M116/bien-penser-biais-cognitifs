# Overconfidence Effect

## Short

Our subjective confidence exceeds the actual accuracy of our knowledge, estimates, or forecasts.

## Example

An expert gives a very narrow range for an economic forecast that depends on many uncertain factors.

## Detailed description

Overconfidence includes at least overestimating one's performance, overplacing oneself relative to others, and excessive precision in confidence intervals. These forms do not always behave in the same way.

## Why it matters

It magnifies risks, planning errors, poor forecasts, and resistance to opposing views.

## Limits and nuances

Its level depends on task difficulty and the definition used; overconfidence should not be conflated with the Dunning-Kruger effect.

## Prevention

- Express confidence as probabilities and regularly check calibration.
- Use wider intervals, base rates, and feedback on past forecasts.
