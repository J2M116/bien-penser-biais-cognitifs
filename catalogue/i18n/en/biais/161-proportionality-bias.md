# Proportionality Bias

## Short

We assume that an event of great magnitude must have a cause of comparable size.

## Example

A major disaster seems to require a vast conspiracy, while a chain of small errors feels psychologically insufficient.

## Detailed description

The intuitive match between the magnitude of an effect and the magnitude of its cause makes some explanations feel more satisfying. It can lead us to overlook complex systems, cumulative effects, thresholds, and rare events.

## Why it matters

It plays a role in causal reasoning, accidents, history, health, and belief in conspiracy theories.

## Limits and nuances

The literature is more limited than it is for anchoring or framing, and a major cause can of course produce a major event.

## Prevention

- Build a detailed causal chain that includes small causes, interactions, and probabilities.
- Compare the proportional account with known mechanisms in similar events.
