# Salience Bias

## Short

Vivid, emotional, or unusual elements carry more weight in judgment than less visible but relevant information.

## Example

A spectacular accident influences safety policy more than thousands of ordinary incidents that caused more casualties in total.

## Detailed description

Salience directs attention and makes recall easier; it can therefore be mistaken for frequency, importance, or causality. Interfaces and media partly determine what becomes salient.

## Why it matters

It operates in almost every presentation of information and interacts with availability, affect, and attention.

## Limits and nuances

Salience is a general mechanism rather than a single test; its effect depends on the task and on what is made visible.

## Prevention

- Define the criteria before seeing the most striking cases.
- Also present denominators, distributions, and less visible information.
