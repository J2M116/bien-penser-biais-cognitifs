# Stereotypical Bias

## Short

We attribute characteristics expected of a person's group rather than relying on relevant individual information.

## Example

Two identical applications are evaluated differently because the suggested gender does not match expectations for the occupation.

## Detailed description

Stereotypes are associations or expectations about groups. They shape attention, interpretation, memory, and decisions, sometimes without conscious intent, and can produce cumulative discrimination.

## Why it matters

Their impact is substantial in hiring, health, justice, education, media, and the design of automated systems.

## Limits and nuances

“Stereotypical bias” is a very broad family; each application-specific account should identify the group, domain, measure, and consequences being studied.

## Prevention

- Structure the criteria and, where possible, conceal irrelevant characteristics.
- Compare decisions across groups and audit disparities using appropriate data.
